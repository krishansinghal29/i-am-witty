# Storytelling Exercises — Rated

> Every exercise in `final/exercise-research.xlsx` → **Master Exercises** that lists **`storytelling`** as one of its domains — **128 exercises** — scored on **Difficulty**, **Fun**, and **Impact on teaching storytelling**. The standout exercises score high on **Fun + Impact**; difficulty tells you *when* to use them, not whether they're good.

## How to read the ratings

**Difficulty** — my judgment of **real demand** (cognitive load + live improvisation + the exposure of performing for others), starting from the catalog's tag and bumping up exercises whose sustained-telling or group-performance load it under-counted:

- 🟢 **Easy** — low load, scaffolded, short, or private: warm-ups, fill-in templates, journaling, concrete-prompt (dice/card) games, one-word/one-line contributions

- 🟡 **Medium** — sustain or improvise a *full* story, perform it for others, or apply a structure/framework

- 🔴 **Hard** — advanced craft frameworks, long multi-session builds, or complex live performance

**Impact** — how directly the exercise builds *core* storytelling craft (structure & arc, story-finding, stakes/suspense, character, show-don't-tell, meaning):

- **High** = load-bearing narrative skill · **Medium** = supports storytelling (delivery, listening, a single technique) · **Low** = peripheral sub-skill

**Fun** — rated **High / Medium / Low**, and tagged with one of 8 *fun-type* archetypes so you can pick by the kind of play you want:


| Fun type | What it feels like | Count |
|---|---|:---:|
| 🎲 **Party Game** | Competitive, rule-driven group games with surprise and stakes — the loudest, most social fun. | 19 |
| ⚡ **Improv Spark** | Spontaneous invention sparked by random prompts, dice, or cards — fast, playful, unpredictable. | 9 |
| 🎭 **Theatrical Play** | Embodiment, character voices, and physical performance — theatrical and expressive. | 19 |
| 🤝 **Heart-to-Heart** | Real sharing, deep listening, and connection — warm, meaningful, lower-key fun. | 19 |
| 🧩 **Imagination Lab** | Imaginative "what-if" fabrication — inventing backstories, perspectives, and worlds. | 12 |
| 🪞 **Inner Mining** | Reflective journaling and life-inventory mining — quiet, introspective, foundational. | 12 |
| 🧱 **Craft Workshop** | Structure analysis and rewriting drills — cerebral, high-learning, low-giggle. | 33 |
| 🎤 **Stage Reps** | Rehearsal, delivery, and memorization reps — disciplined polish for performance. | 5 |

**★ Score** — a quick composite of **Fun + Impact** (each High=3 / Med=2 / Low=1), shown as 1–5 stars. The best all-round exercises sit at ★★★★☆ and above.

> **A note on “best”:** the prompt asked for the exercises that score high on all three. Difficulty isn't a quality axis (a *hard* exercise isn't *better*), so I treat it as a **level/sequencing** signal — re-rated by *real demand*, not the raw catalog tag — and rank “best” by **Fun + Impact**. Below, the top picks are grouped by difficulty so the accessible gems rise to the top — ideal for a low-stakes, play-first app. Every exercise's three ratings are in the full table, so you can re-rank to taste.

## Methodology

- **Source:** `final/exercise-research.xlsx` → sheet **Master Exercises** (993 rows). Filtered to the **128** rows whose `Domains` field includes `storytelling`.

- **Difficulty** is re-rated on **real demand** — cognitive load, live improvisation, and the exposure of performing for others — not the catalog's barrier-to-entry tag. The guiding test is *who does the plotting*: if the exercise hands you the **plot structure** (a fill-in template) or asks only a **tiny per-turn contribution**, or is **private/recalled**, it's Easy; if **you** must construct and tell a *full* story in real time, perform it for others, or apply a framework, it's Medium+. It starts from the sheet's `Difficulty` (beginner→Easy, intermediate→Medium, advanced→Hard), then bumps **11** under-counted exercises **Easy→Medium**:

  - *Sustained / live telling & performance:* Tour Guide (EX-0010), Three Words (EX-0011), Camp Fire (EX-0012), Force of Nature Portrait (EX-0019), Hidden Self Story (EX-0020), Cusp of an Era Story (EX-0022), Story Whoosh (EX-0087).

  - *Live improv games* (ingredients are handed to you, but you plot + tell live — skill targets include improvised plotting / fluency): Story Dice Rolls (EX-0066), Story Stones (EX-0088), Cast of Thousands (EX-0112), Nanofiction Four-Card Build (EX-0113).

  - Staying Easy by the same logic: fill-in templates that supply the plot (Story Spine), one-word/one-line circle games (Group Story, Fortunately Unfortunately, I Am a Tree), recalled short tellings, and *solo, revisable* invention with no real-time pressure (People Watching, Object Autobiography).

- **Fun** and **Impact** are editorial judgments based on each exercise's description, skill targets, format (solo/pair/group), and origin. Fun weighs social play, surprise, embodiment, and low-stakes enjoyment; Impact weighs how central the trained skill is to *constructing and telling* a story (vs. supporting or peripheral skills).

- **Scope:** Fun is rated for a *general* audience (objective playfulness), **not** tuned to a specific persona — so loud/competitive group games rank high even though they'd feel high-stakes to an anxious introvert. Impact reflects **craft-centrality** (how core the skill is to building a story), independent of how repeatable or delivery-focused the exercise is.

- Difficulty split: 🟢 Easy ×54, 🟡 Medium ×69, 🔴 Hard ×5.

- **★ Score = Fun + Impact** (High=3, Med=2, Low=1), 1–5 stars. Difficulty is reported but intentionally excluded from the score (see note above).

- Distribution: ★★★★★ ×2, ★★★★☆ ×49, ★★★☆☆ ×61, ★★☆☆☆ ×15, ★☆☆☆☆ ×1.
