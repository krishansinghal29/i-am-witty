## Search queries that worked
- Naming a specific format and asking for its drills: "competitive debate practice
  drills ... refutation exercises", "Model UN training exercises moderated caucus".
- Format-name queries: "PechaKucha ... 20 slides 20 seconds", "Just A Minute ...
  hesitation repetition deviation".
- Pedagogy-source queries: "Toastmasters meeting roles ... Grammarian word of the day",
  "progymnasmata commonplace comparison synkrisis introduction of law".
- "improv warmups for public speakers", "applied/business improv games presentation
  skills" — surfaced the speaker-adapted improv games cleanly.
- Mining a found page's drill names back into new searches (e.g., the debate-learning
  drill list pointed to flowing, cross-ex, spreading queries).

## Sources found but not fully mined (leads for a future pass)
- **scienceofpeople.com vocal warm-ups** and **write-out-loud.com word-games /
  public-speaking-activities** returned 403 / connection-refused to the fetcher; their
  named exercises were partially recovered from search snippets but the full step lists
  weren't extracted. Likely a few more vocal and word-game drills there.
- **Improv Encyclopedia / improwiki / Improv Notes** full game archives — hundreds of
  games; only the clearly speaking-relevant ones were pulled. Many pure-physical or
  scene-only games were intentionally skipped as belonging to the improv domain.
- **NSDA "Speech Warm-Ups" PDF** is an image/vector PDF the fetcher couldn't read;
  named warmups (e.g., "Arms Out") weren't extracted.
- **Book TOCs** (Talk Like TED, Resonate, The Quick and Easy Way to Effective Speaking,
  Demosthenes/Cicero rhetorica) — exercise-bearing chapters not individually mined; the
  classical and TED material is represented but a chapter-by-chapter pass could add more.
- **Non-English traditions** beyond Greco-Roman: Indian elocution/extempore/JAM-session
  competition culture surfaced but is thin in distinct *drills*; Japanese (PechaKucha is
  the one captured), and rhetorical traditions in other languages largely untouched.

## Honest coverage gaps / under-covered sub-areas
- **Gesture and stage-movement specifics** — captured Mirror Gesture Sync, Newspaper
  Floor, stage-zones, Grounded Stance, Conduct the Speaker; but fine-grained gesture
  vocabularies and blocking/choreography drills are still light.
- **Visual-aid / slide-craft delivery drills** beyond PowerPoint Karaoke and PechaKucha
  (e.g., assertion-evidence slides, B-key blackout practice, laser-pointer discipline).
- **Virtual / on-camera speaking** (webcam eye-line, framing, energy-through-the-lens,
  hybrid-audience handling) — barely touched; a rich modern sub-area.
- **Panel / moderation / interview-the-guest skills** — partly via Character Interviews
  and Fishbowl, but moderating a panel and being interviewed are under-drilled.
- **Sales/pitch and negotiation persuasion** — Elevator Pitch, Rapid-Fire Objection
  Handling, Make a Commercial cover the basics; deeper consultative-selling and
  storytelling-for-pitch drills remain.
- **Accent/clarity for non-native speakers** sits mostly in the voice-tonality domain and
  was left there.

## Stopping rationale
Final search waves (elocution/declamation, memory systems, posture, classroom game
listicles, rare debate drills) were returning predominantly variants of exercises
already captured — story-starters, one-minute speeches, gibberish, power poses,
PREP-style frameworks — indicating genuine diminishing returns for distinctly-named,
distinctly-procedured exercises within this domain's scope.
