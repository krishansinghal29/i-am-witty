(Carnegie, Lowndes, Cabane, Duhigg, Gottman, Imago, DBT, MI, Crucial Conversations STATE,
Aron's 36 Questions, basic improv games, ESL icebreakers). This run deliberately pushed
into adjacent traditions the prior agent barely touched:

  OFNR translation/Says games, self-empathy aloud, hard-to-hear slips, Day of Needs,

## Richest sources

1. **NVCWiki "Practice group exercises"** (en.nvcwiki.com) — a dense list of named NVC
   drills (OFNR Says, Jackalapalooza, Day of Needs, hard-to-hear slips, empathy-vs-non-
   empathy). Many more partly-detailed games here than I mined.
2. **Authentic Relating Games corpus** (authrev.org "what are AR games", relatingarts.com,
   Circling Institute) — Noticing, Truths, Watermelon, Would You, Circling. The full
   250+-game "Relating Games Manual" and the Authentic World Games Night Handbook PDF are
   the motherlode (see gaps).
3. **Improv repositories** (improvencyclopedia.org, improvgames.com, learnimprov.com,
   ImprovDr, Beat by Beat Press) — endless conversation-relevant games; I took the ones
   most clearly about listening/status/co-construction.
4. **Liberating Structures** (liberatingstructures.com + the "Engaging Everyone" handbook
   PDF) — 33+ structured dialogue methods; I took the most conversation-skill-relevant.
5. **CBT/REBT social-anxiety material** (National Social Anxiety Center, Albert Ellis
   Institute, Social Anxiety Alliance UK, Therapist Aid) — exposure hierarchies, social
   mishap experiments, shame-attacking, rejection therapy.

Honorable mentions: Greater Good in Action (Berkeley) for research-backed micro-practices;
Center for Building a Culture of Empathy (empathy circles); ESL teaching sites (teach-this,
eslinfo, FluentU, americanenglish.state.gov) for structured speaking formats; charisma
coaching (Art of Manliness, Art of Charm, Science of People); Chris Voss / Crucial
Conversations / Difficult Conversations summaries.

## Search queries that worked well

- Naming a specific framework + "practice/exercise/drill/roleplay/worksheet" (e.g.
  "crucial conversations contrasting CRIB practice exercise", "tactical empathy label
  mirror calibrated question drill", "Manuel Smith fogging negative inquiry").
- Listing several known game names in one query as anchors (improv games "alphabet game"
  "yes lets" "convergence" "ding" "new choice") — surfaced repositories fast.
- Tradition-name + "steps how to run / facilitation rules" (Bohm dialogue, empathy circle,
  circling, talking stick).
- "filetype-style" handbook hunts (NVCWiki practice group exercises; Liberating Structures
  Engaging Everyone handbook) pulled the densest single lists.

## Honest coverage gaps / under-covered sub-areas

- **Cross-cultural / non-English-origin traditions**: still thin. Captured Indigenous
  council/talking-stick and (Western-mediated) NVC, but Japanese *honne/tatemae* or
  *nemawashi* practice drills, *majlis*/Arab hospitality conversation norms, African
  palaver/indaba formats, and structured dialogue from other languages are essentially
  uncovered. Likely real exercises exist but are hard to surface in English search.
- **Neurodiversity-specific conversation drills** (autism social-skills curricula like
  PEERS, conversation "scripts"/social stories) — barely touched; a whole sub-literature.
- **Phone/voice-call and async/text conversation drills** — captured a little (follow-up
  note), but texting-cadence, voice-message, and video-call-specific practice is light.
- **Professional-context micro-skills** — sales discovery-call drills, therapeutic
  alliance micro-skills beyond Egan/MI, customer-service de-escalation scripts — partly
  in (OARS, tactical empathy) but not exhaustively.
- The two big gated/erroring corpora (Authentic World handbook PDF; authrev 250-game
  manual) are the single biggest known unmined seam; a follow-up run with PDF access would
  likely add 20-40 more distinct AR/circling games.

Diminishing returns reached: by the final two waves, most results were variants of
already-captured exercises (more icebreakers, more "active listening games" lists
recycling Telephone/back-to-back/paraphrase, more ESL speaking-activity roundups).
