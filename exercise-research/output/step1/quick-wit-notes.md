## Richest sources (best yield of distinct, well-specified exercises)
1. **The Practice Space debate drills page** — eight tight, distinct quick-thinking
   drills (Justify It, Blow Up the Balloon, Wordsmith, Take Action, Pulled from the
   Headlines, Quick Outlines, Talking Code) that are NOT the usual improv canon.
   (practice-space.org/debate_guide/resource-13)
2. **Improv game libraries** — ImprovDr.com, Improv Encyclopedia, improwiki,
   learnimprov.com, Sacramento Comedy Spot warmup list, New York Improv Theater.
   Deep, well-described, and cross-linked; great for mining new names as queries.
3. **BBC "I'm Sorry I Haven't a Clue" panel-game canon** — Cheddar Gorge, Uxbridge
   English Dictionary, Late Arrivals, One Song to the Tune of Another. A whole
   tradition of structured verbal-wit games the prior agent missed entirely.
4. **Freestyle rap pedagogy** — beatstorapon.com, ColeMize Studios, 99beats,
   Max Deutsch's 30-day freestyle experiment. Yielded Rhyme Fasting, Rhyming Word
   Ladder, Eight Bars No-Naming — drills that isolate sub-skills of fast speech.
5. **Classical rhetoric progymnasmata** (rhetoric.byu.edu / Silva Rhetoricae) —
   the Thesis and Chreia exercises are 2,000-year-old impromptu both-sides and
   instant-elaboration scaffolds, very much "quick-wit" though rarely framed so.

Honorable mentions: Vancouver Debate Academy (Shadow Debate, Get the Gist), ESL/TEFL
fluency activity collections (FluentU, Teach-This, EnglishClub) for Describe-and-Draw
and Running Dictation, and banter/crowd-work coaching (jaunty.org, Funny Muscle,
Patrick King material) for Agree-and-Amplify, Observational Riff, Callback, Comparison.

## Queries that worked well
- Mining specific game NAMES as queries (e.g. `"Cheddar Gorge"`, `"Uxbridge English
  Dictionary"`, `"Dr Know It All" one word at a time`) — highest hit rate by far.
- `filetype`-free but source-targeted: `practice-space debate drills warm-ups`,
  `Vancouver debate solo drills`.
- Tradition-anchored queries: `progymnasmata chreia thesis impromptu both sides`,
  `British panel show games Mornington Crescent verbal improv`.
- Sub-skill isolation queries for rap: `freestyle "continuous rhyme" "stream of
  consciousness" "describe the room"` surfaced Rhyme Fasting and the no-naming drill.
- Banter mechanics by name: `agree and amplify reframe ready response banter`.

## Sources found but not fully mined (coverage gaps)
- **Full improv encyclopedias** (Improv Encyclopedia, improwiki, ImprovDr master
  index, Texas Thespians DOC, Ike Butler PDF) contain hundreds more short-form games;
  I captured the speed/verbal-agility-relevant ones but many remain. Most are scene
  games rather than pure quick-wit, hence deprioritized.
- **Non-English-origin verbal games** beyond shiritori/Game of Cities (already in
  file) and the ISIHAC canon: likely rich material in other traditions (e.g. Persian
  *bayt-bazi* / Antakshari poetry chains, Arabic *zajal* improvised verse duels,
  Filipino *balagtasan* debate-in-verse) — surfaced only tangentially; worth a
  dedicated pass.
- **Competitive debate format-specific drills** (Lincoln-Douglas, BP, Policy
  "flowing" and "rebuttal redo" drills) — captured the generic ones; format-specific
  speed drills (e.g. spreading practice, POI drills) under-covered.
- **Slam poetry / spoken-word** warmups and **cypher** culture beyond rap — partially
  covered via freestyle; spoken-word-specific quick-composition drills under-mined.
- **YouTube transcripts** (freestyle coaches, crowd-work breakdowns) not directly
  fetched; titles suggest more named drills exist behind video content.
- **Auctioneer / sportscaster / "speed talking"** vocal-agility traditions touched
  only via News Reporter Narration; could yield more.
