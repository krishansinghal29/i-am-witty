# Step 2b Provider Summaries — Batch: community-1

Batch theme: community / open-source orgs, podcasts, and books focused on
storytelling, impromptu speaking, and improv. These are the canonical
non-app exercise corpora — high value for step-3 exercise extraction.

---

## Toastmasters International
- **Methodology**: Structured, peer-evaluated rep-based curriculum. The Pathways
  program is 11 learning paths x 5 levels x 14+ projects (43 distinct projects
  total), each a graded speech assignment delivered live and evaluated by a club
  member. Layered on top is the meeting machinery (Table Topics, Topicsmaster,
  evaluator roles) that runs every session.
- **Exercises found**: 18 logged (Table Topics + 17 named Pathways projects most
  relevant to our domains; the full catalog is 43 projects).
- **Evidence quality**: Mostly confirmed — full project descriptions and lengths
  pulled from district-published Pathways catalogs that mirror official docs.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: Table Topics (canonical impromptu/quick-wit drill),
  Power of Humor in an Impromptu Speech, Q&A Session, Managing a Difficult
  Audience, Connect with Storytelling.
- **2c deep scrape?** YES. The full 43-project Pathways matrix with exact
  objectives is the single richest structured oratory program in this batch.
  Start URL: https://westsidetoastmasters.com/education/Pathways-Paths-and-Projects-Catalog-V2.1.pdf
  (note: PDF needs OCR/text extraction, not WebFetch) and
  https://d4tm.org/toastmasters-pathways/paths-and-projects

## The Moth
- **Methodology**: Performance-as-practice. The StorySLAM open-mic competition is
  itself the practice structure (true, personal, 5-min, no notes, scored live).
  Surrounded by a teachable craft framework (stakes, change, start-in-action,
  opening line) and a consumer card game that ports the format home.
- **Exercises found**: 10 — 1 live format, 5 craft drills, 4 card-game modes.
- **Evidence quality**: Mostly confirmed — tips page, events page, and the
  card-game product page are all primary sources. The education-program Story Map
  / oral-drafting flow is inferred from blog + third-party teaching write-ups.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: The 4 "A Game of Storytelling" modes are directly
  portable to an app (theme cards, 3-word pitch, vote), and the craft drills
  (stakes / change / opening line) are clean writing-prompt exercises.
- **2c deep scrape?** MEDIUM. The card game (100 themes + 300 prompts) is a
  strong prompt corpus but isn't fully published online. Start URL:
  https://themoth.org/share-your-story/storytelling-tips-tricks and the
  "A Game of Storytelling" product page https://themoth.org/a-game-of-storytelling

## Stanford GSB / Matt Abrahams — Think Fast Talk Smart
- **Methodology**: Academic / cognitive-behavioral approach to spontaneous
  speaking. Six-step method (manage anxiety, ditch perfectionism, stay open,
  listen, structure, be succinct) plus reusable response frameworks
  (problem-solution-benefit; what / so what / now what). Borrows improv warm-ups
  (Shout the Wrong Name) from his Stanford co-taught course.
- **Exercises found**: 5 — strongest are Shout the Wrong Name (a genuine drill),
  the structure-framework drill, and the Anxiety Management Plan.
- **Evidence quality**: Confirmed at the conceptual level (publisher page, GSB
  articles, podcast). The book's printable per-chapter exercises are referenced
  but the individual exercise texts are paywalled in the book itself.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: Framework-based impromptu answering and Shout the
  Wrong Name map almost 1:1 to quick-wit app drills.
- **2c deep scrape?** NO (web). Most extractable value is inside the book, not on
  the free site (fastersmarter.io homepage exposes only episode lists). If we want
  the exact exercises, source the book rather than scraping. Free resources start:
  https://www.fastersmarter.io/

## Improv Encyclopedia / Improwiki
- **Methodology**: Not a program — an open reference corpus of 500+ improv/drama
  games and exercises across ~30 categories (icebreakers, warm-ups, spontaneity,
  trust, long-form). No curriculum or feedback; pure exercise inventory.
- **Exercises found**: 14 logged here (the verbal/quick-wit subset), out of 500+
  available — this is the largest single extraction target in the batch.
- **Evidence quality**: Confirmed — every game has its own published instruction
  page on the wiki.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: Nearly all logged games are quick-wit/verbal-agility
  gold: Questions Only, Word at a Time, Alphabet Game, Free Association, Last
  Letter, Rhymes, Press Conference, Story Story Die. Many are 1-2 player and adapt
  to AI-roleplay or solo drill formats.
- **2c deep scrape?** YES — highest priority in this batch. Scrape the full game
  index and category pages for instructions + suggestions. Start URL:
  https://improvencyclopedia.org/games/ (and per-game pages linked from it).

## Speak Up Storytelling podcast (Matthew & Elysha Dicks)
- **Methodology**: Storytelling craft via the Storyworthy method. Core engine is
  Homework for Life (daily noticing/journaling to build a story bank), plus
  generative drills (First Last Best Worst, Crash and Burn) and a per-episode
  workshop loop where the hosts dissect a real story for stakes, change, and
  delivery.
- **Exercises found**: 4 — all clean, app-portable practice activities.
- **Evidence quality**: Confirmed — Homework for Life is documented on Dicks' own
  site; the other drills are well-attested across multiple Storyworthy summaries.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: Homework for Life and First/Last/Best/Worst are
  excellent daily-habit and conversation-seed exercises that fit a play-driven
  app perfectly.
- **2c deep scrape?** MEDIUM. The named exercises are already captured; deeper
  value is in the Storyworthy book, not the podcast feed. If scraping, the
  matthewdicks.com Homework for Life page is the cleanest source:
  https://matthewdicks.com/homework-for-life/

## Radical Agreement Project
- **Methodology**: Free daily live drop-in improv classes (weekdays 4pm ET),
  beginner-focused, scene-based with end-of-scene instructor feedback. A
  rep-plus-coaching loop rather than a documented curriculum.
- **Exercises found**: 2 — both inferred and generic (daily workshop, scene +
  feedback). No specific named drills are published.
- **Evidence quality**: Inferred — the site describes format and pricing but not
  individual exercises; details come from the class-info page and a review.
- **Most relevant to riffy**: Low direct extraction value (no published drill
  list), but a useful live-practice benchmark and a possible observation target
  if we want to inventory which exercises a real beginner class actually runs.
- **2c deep scrape?** NO. Nothing structured to scrape; the only way to get the
  exercise list would be live observation of a session. Start URL (for reference):
  https://www.radicalagreement.com/free-improv-classes
