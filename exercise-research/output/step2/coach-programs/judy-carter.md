## 1. Who the coach is

**Judy Carter** is a Los Angeles–based comedian, comedy teacher, and humorous
keynote speaker who has run the **Hollywood Comedy Workshops** (Los Angeles and
New York) for decades and says she has coached **5,000+ comics**. She is the
author of three widely cited how-to books:

- **_Stand-Up Comedy: The Book_** (1989) — her first manual.
- **_The Comedy Bible: From Stand-Up to Sitcom_** (Simon & Schuster / Fireside,
  2001) — "as seen on the Oprah Winfrey Show," ~367 pages, the master text.
- **_The NEW Comedy Bible: The Ultimate Guide to Writing and Performing Stand-Up
  Comedy_** (2020) — a stand-up-focused rewrite that adds **"48 new exercises to
  turn problems into punchlines."**
- Plus **_The Comedy Bible Workbook_** (2020), the interactive companion to the
  New Comedy Bible.

She has since pivoted much of her business to **speaking / "the message of you"**
(book: _The Message of You_) and **corporate humor**, but the stand-up method is
the core of her brand.

**Why she matters for this project:** Carter's system is the most explicitly
**therapeutic and topic-first** of the major comedy methods. Her thesis — *"stand-up
is the art of turning problems into punchlines"* — means every exercise starts
from the writer's own pain, fear, shame, or frustration, then runs it through a
small number of named **comedy formulas** (act-out, mix, list of three, reluctant
admission, etc.). This makes her unusually well-suited to a self-improvement /
anxiety-adjacent app: the on-ramp is "what's bugging you?", not "be funny." Her
material is also delivered as a literal **day-by-day workbook** with fill-in-the-blank
formula templates, which maps almost 1:1 onto runnable solo exercises.

**Audience:** absolute beginners and "funny people who've never written a joke,"
working comics honing material, and — increasingly — **public speakers,
executives, and corporate audiences** who want to be funnier and less stressed
("Comedy Workshop for Business: Turning Problems Into Punchlines").

## 3. The methodology (theory of improvement)

Carter's system rests on a handful of load-bearing claims:

1. **Stand-up is the art of turning problems into punchlines.** You don't search
   for "what's funny" — you start from your own **pain**: what you *hate, what
   annoys you, what you worry about, what you're afraid of, what you're ashamed
   of*. Comedy is, in her framing, **self-healing**: "feel and see the pain, then
   choose to laugh in its face." This is the emotional engine of the whole method.

2. **Don't think "funny" — think weird / stupid / scary / hard.** Her signature
   reframe: a topic has one of **four basic attitudes — weird, scary, hard,
   stupid.** Instead of straining to be funny, you say *"(topic) is (weird /
   scary / hard / stupid) because…"* and **rant**. "Thinking in terms of what is
   weird, stupid, scary, or hard, rather than what is funny, will free your
   creative process."

3. **The joke formula = serious setup + funny execution.** Carter breaks a joke
   into: **Attitude → Topic → Premise → Act-Out → Mix → Act-Out.** The **attitude
   + topic + premise** is the (serious, truthful, specific) **setup**; the
   **act-out + mix** is where the laughs live. "Comedy isn't meant to be read.
   It's meant to be performed. The laughs are in the execution of the act-outs."

4. **Premise, not description.** "A premise is not a description of what happened.
   It's a cut-to-the-chase, get-to-the-point, original observation," and it must be
   "truthful and relatable." **"Get specific — funny is in the details."**

5. **Act it out, don't tell it.** The **act-out** is her highest-value move:
   instead of *describing* a person/thing in the setup, you **become** it and play
   the scene. Many of her exercises explicitly say "reveal at the end" (withhold
   the key context — age, location, who's talking — until the punch).

6. **Mixes generate punchlines.** A **mix** = a comparison or a context-swap.
   Compare any two things (Before vs Now, Them vs Me, Men vs Women), or **change
   the environment** (put your self-absorbed mother in a different job, e.g. a
   suicide hotline) to expose the funny.

7. **It's a numbers game; write every day with a buddy.** "The more pages you
   have, the more likely you are to hit on truly inventive stuff." Hence the
   **Morning Writings** (30 prompts), the **"Living Funny" daily exercises**, the
   **Seinfeld-style write-every-day** discipline ("your funny bone is a muscle"),
   and the **Comedy Buddy** — you partner up and work the book together to beat
   fear and procrastination, because "very few comics create material alone."

8. **Then the polish & business.** Apply named cleanup moves — **tags** (extra
   punches off one setup), the **three-laughs rule** (a joke that can't earn three
   laughs gets reworked or cut), **callbacks**, **topic runs** (grouping jokes by
   topic), cutting clunkers — order a **set list**, and finally turn material into
   money (one-person shows, sitcom spec scripts, "funny money," and **Punching
   Up** at the powerful, never down).

In short: **pain in → attitude/rant → premise → act-out/mix → tag & polish →
perform → sell.** Make the *topic* personal and specific, attach an attitude,
then run it through a small set of templated formulas, every day, with a buddy.

### 4c. The core joke-building pipeline (CONFIRMED method)

1. **List your problems** — what you hate / what annoys you / what you worry about
   / what you're afraid of / what you're ashamed of. (Topic mining from pain.)
2. **Pick a topic and assign an attitude** — *(topic) is weird / scary / hard /
   stupid.*
3. **Rant the "because…"** — speak (don't write) about *why* it's weird/scary/
   hard/stupid; or interrogate it with **"Why is it weird? Why is it stupid? Why
   is it hard? Why is it scary?"**
4. **Write a premise** — a sharp, specific, truthful, relatable observation (not a
   description), ~1–2 lines.
5. **Find the act-out** — become a person/thing in the premise and play the
   moment; often "reveal at the end."
6. **Add a mix** — a comparison or an environment-swap for an extra angle.
7. **Tag it** — pile more punches on the same setup; apply the **three-laughs
   rule**.
8. **Run topics, cut clunkers, build a set list, perform, review.**

### 4d. Named formula exercises (CONFIRMED from the free first-party workbook)

These are the discrete, templated "Comedy Formula" drills in Carter's public
Stand-Up Comedy Workbook (each has a fill-in worksheet):

- **Making Fun of Yourself** — list ≥3 things people joke about you behind your
  back; circle the most embarrassing.
- **Reluctant Admission** — a 3-beat rhythm: a **bold boastful "lie"** (loud) →
  **Admission One** (quieter, walks it back) → **Admission Two** (soft, embarrassed,
  the real/pathetic truth) → snap back **"Anyway!"** (loud).
- **Half-and-Half Mashup** — "You may not know this, but I'm half ___ and half
  ___, and that means I ___." (Fill from your ethnicity, parents' nationalities,
  hobbies, jobs.)
- **Re-defining Frustrations** — "You may not know this, but [industry term] is
  actually a [language] term, meaning [your real frustration]." (e.g. *entrepreneur
  = French for "I work in my underwear"*).
- **Acronyms** — rework a real acronym from your job so each letter spells out
  your frustrations (e.g. FORD = "Found On Road Dead").
- **List of 3 (Rule of Three)** — the third item in any list is the funny one;
  practice with explicit rhythm patterns (big/big/small, small/small/big,
  obvious/obvious/weird, subtle/subtle/obvious).
- **Acting-Out Premises** — given a premise, invent an **act-out** for it; several
  prompts specify **"reveal at the end"** (withhold age/location/speaker until the
  punch).
- **Comparison (Mix) Jokes** — three sub-templates: **Before vs Now**, **Them vs
  Me**, **Men vs Women**, plus environment-swap mixes.

### 4e. Performance, persona & career (method, lightly drilled)

- **Persona / POV / "the message of you":** build an authentic comic voice from
  your real self and vulnerabilities (vs. a put-on character); "authenticity is
  the secret sauce."
- **Crowd work / save lines / handling the room** (workshop-taught; few public
  step-by-steps).
- **Punching Up** — aim jokes at the rich/powerful, never down at vulnerable
  groups (Commandment 6).
- **Work "clean"** for corporate; reserve explicit material for clubs.
- **Pay your dues / network / social-media presence** — career discipline
  (Commandments).
